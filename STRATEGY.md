# Strategic Framing

## The Core Misdiagnosis

The existing roadmap treats Everhope as an e-commerce store with a conversion problem.
It is not. It is a clinical trust problem that happens to sit inside an e-commerce interface.

Every person on this site is a cancer patient or their caregiver. The purchase decision
is not governed by consumer preference — it is governed by clinical anxiety. "Will this
supplement interfere with my chemotherapy?" is not a question a better checkout flow answers.

The existing roadmap addresses this partially:
- AI chatbot on PDP answers questions reactively (user has to ask)
- PDP overhaul adds static clinical evidence and doctor quotes
- EHR → WhatsApp flow is coordinator-driven, hospital-scoped

What remains unaddressed: proactive clinical permission at the moment of purchase intent,
and recurring clinical data as a systematic purchase trigger.

## The Three Unexplored Pillars

### 1. Diagnostic Commerce
Cancer patients generate clinical data on a 2-4 week cycle throughout treatment —
CBC panels, albumin, ferritin, Vitamin D, B12. None of this data currently connects
to purchase intent. Every bloodwork report is a clinical signal that maps directly
to supplement need. Everhope has the oncology expertise to read that signal.
No one in India's supplement market is doing this. It creates a recurring external
trigger for purchase that does not depend on the user remembering to return.

### 2. Expert-Mediated Distribution
The 25+ experts on the site are trust badges, not distribution agents.
The EHR → WhatsApp flow serves hospital coordinators. Individual oncologists
and oncology nutritionists — who have their own patient panels across any clinic —
have no tool to recommend Everhope products to their patients directly.
A prescribe-link mechanism turns every expert's consultation into a potential
pre-filled cart for their patient. This bypasses the discovery-to-PDP funnel
entirely for referred patients.

### 3. Caregiver Commerce
The site is architected entirely for the patient. Caregivers represent an estimated
30-40% of buyers in this category. They have different psychology (buying for someone
in crisis), different payment behavior (skew away from COD toward UPI/card), and
different information needs. The current architecture does not acknowledge them
as a distinct buyer segment. A caregiver-mode checkout is a separate conversion
funnel, not a UX variation.

## What This Roadmap Is Not

It is not incremental improvement to the existing plan.
It is not CRO. It is not more trust badges or faster checkout.

It is the infrastructure that makes clinical data, expert relationships,
and caregiver behavior into systematic commerce mechanisms — none of which
the existing roadmap touches.
